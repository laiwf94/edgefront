# Changelog

Changes to edgefront, newest first. Each week's entries name the roadmap issue they close or advance.

## Unreleased

### Cache

- Streaming fill. A storable response that declares a length over 64 KiB is streamed to its client while it arrives, and to every collapsed client through a shared growing body (`edgefront_cache::fill::Filling`). The flight stays open until the body is in (`Lead::publish_held`), so late arrivals join. A client that arrived mid-fill got its first byte within 50 ms of the origin's first byte. An origin that fails partway, sends a body of the wrong length or times out aborts every reader, and nothing is stored. Followers with conditionals or a range wait for the whole object. (#90)
- The disk tier is a log-structured segment store (`edgefront_cache::store::segment`) in place of one file per URL. Records are appended to segment files with a checksummed frame, removals and purges append tombstones, and the segments' total is held to the budget by deleting the oldest segment. When the write queue is empty, the oldest segment is compacted once less than half of it is live. New metrics: `cachet_disk_segment_bytes`, `cachet_disk_segments`, and the write amplification as `cachet_disk_written_bytes_total` over `cachet_disk_user_bytes_total`, with `cachet_disk_compacted_bytes_total`. A torn last frame is cut off at startup, and every read re-checks its checksums. Files of the old layout are removed, which empties that tier once. (#90)
- A clean shutdown writes the index to `index.snap` (`edgefront_cache::store::index`). The next start checks it against the segments and replays only what came after. One million objects were ready in 305 ms in a release build on a 16-core arm64 Mac, and the bench workflow holds that test to under 1 s. After a crash, the snapshot is gone and the store scans. (#90)
- Hits and fills answer up to 16 byte ranges. Overlapping or nearly adjacent ranges are coalesced. One remaining range is a plain 206, several are `multipart/byteranges`, and unsatisfiable members are dropped. More than 16 ranges, or ranges adding up to more than twice the object before coalescing, get the whole object with 200. `If-Match` (strong comparison) and `If-Unmodified-Since` are now evaluated on hits and give 412, in RFC 9110 section 13.2.2's order before `If-None-Match`, `If-Modified-Since` and `Range`. The `range` fuzz target checks the new invariants. `make difftest-cache` no longer sends multiple ranges, since cachet 0.1 answered them with 200 and this is a deliberate difference. (#90)

- The `bench` workflow's `gate` job runs on pull requests and on demand. It builds the candidate and the baseline commit and runs `bench/ci/gate.toml` (20 s of hits on one CPU) five times each, alternating the two on the same runner, then judges the result. Calibration on 18 recording runs showed that the hosted runners' CPU models differ by up to 60%, and that one model's run medians move by up to 27%. Against committed samples, two unchanged runs failed. The same-runner comparison passed every unchanged run. (#87)
- `edgefront_bench::gate` is the benchmark regression gate. Each metric (response-time p99, and completed requests per CPU second of the target) fails only when two things hold: a one-sided Mann-Whitney U test finds the candidate worse at p < 0.01, and the worse median exceeds a 5% tolerance. The test is exact while there are at most 250,000 orderings and uses the corrected normal approximation beyond that. Fixed ceilings cap the binary and image sizes. `bench/gates.toml` holds the thresholds and the calibration behind them. `bench/baseline/gate.json` keeps the recorded samples per CPU model. `edgefront-bench gate` writes a JSON verdict naming each failing metric, plus a Markdown summary. `edgefront-bench baseline` records samples, and `run --repeat N` alternates N runs across the targets. (#87)

- `edgefront_bench::report` writes one result per scenario and target, `<out>/<scenario>/<target>.json`, held to `bench/schema/result.schema.json`: throughput, p50 to p99.9 of response and service time, the hit ratio counted at the origin, errors, peak RSS and memory, CPU seconds and image size. - `edgefront_bench::measure` reads what a target uses from its cgroup v2: peak RSS (`anon + file_mapped`, sampled every 100 ms, summed over its containers), `memory.peak`, CPU seconds over the measured phase and the effective CPU set, which a run checks against the one it pinned. Images are measured with `docker image inspect`. (#84)
- `edgefront_bench::scenario` reads benchmark scenarios from TOML. A scenario sets the warm-up and a constant or stepped load, the protocol, connections and timeout, the popular objects (count and Zipf exponent), a weighted object size mix and a hit ratio target. Requests are `GET /obj/<size>/<name>`, and each one depends only on the seed and its sequence number. Warm-up asks only for popular objects. After warm-up, a solved share of requests goes to objects asked for once, so the expected hit ratio meets the target against a cache that keeps everything. The expectation comes from a closed form over the Zipf probabilities and matches a replay to within a percentage point. A target the popular objects cannot reach is refused, as are unknown fields and out-of-range values. `Scenario::working_set` gives the bytes a target needs to hold them. Five scenarios are checked in under `bench/scenarios/`: `cache-hit`, `web-mix`, `large-objects`, `miss-heavy` and `ramp` (stepped, HTTP/2). The harness takes serde and toml from the workspace, which adds no direct dependency. (#84)
- `crates/edgefront-bench` is the shared benchmark harness, starting with its open-loop load generator (`edgefront_bench::load`). A `Schedule` of constant or stepped `Phase`s, with an optional warm-up, sets when each request is due; workers send over HTTP/1.1 (one request at a time per connection) or HTTP/2 (multiplexed streams), replacing failed connections. Every phase records a response time from the request's due time, corrected for coordinated omission, and a service time from when it was written, each in an HDR histogram (`edgefront_bench::histogram`, three significant digits from 1 ns to an hour). It also counts status classes, body bytes and connect, timeout and other errors. A 100 ms server stall at 500 requests per second over two connections lifts response-time p99 above 80 ms while service-time p99 stays below it. The histogram is written in the crate rather than taken from hdrhistogram, because the workspace is at its 14-dependency budget. Only the harness enables hyper's `http2` feature. (#84)
- `tests/e2e_cache_layer.rs` runs two edge nodes over real TCP. Each node is the proxy with `CacheLayer` on its `/cached/` route and nothing on `/`, plus an admin listener on `edgefront_core::admin::Router` with `POST /purge` and the signed peer endpoint. The test checks misses, then hits, on each node and the uncached route reaching the origin every time. A tag purge on one node removes the copy on both, and the next request on each is a fresh fetch. `benches/layer_overhead.rs` and `docs/benchmarks/layer.md` give the layer's cost. A bypassing route adds no measurable time, and `filter::run` adds 44 ns. A 4 KiB hit takes 1.44 µs in-process against cachet 0.1's 1.70 µs. End to end with oha, hits run at 150,700 req/s against 143,100. `examples/load.sh` takes `CACHET_BIN` to load another build. (#81)
- cachet's node serves its cache through `CacheLayer`. `src/proxy.rs` is now a front: loop, target-length, host and body checks, then the layer's phases with the origin pool as the upstream. cachet's own caching code is gone, and `tests/cluster.rs`, `tests/revalidation.rs`, `tests/proxy.rs` and `tests/failure.rs` pass unchanged against the layered node. `make difftest-cache` finds 0 divergences from cachet 0.1 over four runs of 50,000 requests. The filter API gains `Upstream::Resend`, at most once per request, which the layer uses to fetch in full after a `304` for another representation, as cachet did. Proxy resends keep the first try's deadline and take a `max_requests` place. The layer also gains an `Outcome` on every response, an `Observer` for metrics, and `LayerSettings::via`. A `HEAD` answered from a copy `max-stale` accepts reports a plain `hit`, as cachet 0.1 did. (#81)
- `edgefront_proxy::server::Proxy::with_filters` runs each route's filter in the request path. The request phase runs after routing and before `max_requests`, so hits need no permit. The upstream response is seen before the request ID is added. Deliver runs on every response, the proxy's own errors included. `Decision::Refresh` answers at once and sends the request upstream in the background, under the cluster's limit and timeout. `CacheLayer` gains request collapsing (`Collapser::lead_or_follow`: followers answer from the leader's stored object or share its failure, and go upstream alone when the result was only for the leader) and the background `stale-while-revalidate` refresh, one per URL. `crates/edgefront-proxy/tests/filters.rs` and `crates/edgefront-cache/tests/proxy_layer.rs` check both over real TCP. (#81)
- `edgefront_core::filter` defines the request phases a layer runs in on a proxy route: request (answer or forward, with the upstream headers editable), upstream response and deliver. Deliver also sees the proxy's own errors. `RouteFilters` attaches a filter per route, so a route without one pays a single branch, and `filter::run` drives the phases around an upstream call that returns its response with its `Delivery`. `edgefront_cache::layer::CacheLayer` is the cache as such a filter with each route's `RoutePolicy`: lookup and hits, conditional fills without the client's conditionals, `304` refresh, storing under the request's purge epoch, streaming bodies over the route's size limit, invalidation by unsafe methods, `stale-if-error` over upstream and proxy failures, and `Cache-Status`. Routes cap lifetimes with `max_ttl_secs` and variants with `max_variants` (`Cache::store_limited`). Not yet in the layer: request collapsing and the background `stale-while-revalidate` refresh. `Prefixed::size_hint` no longer panics when the rest of the body has an exact length. (#81)

- ACME renewal and the managed certificate (`edgefront_core::acme::renew` and `acme::managed`). `Renewer` issues at once, then at two thirds of each certificate's lifetime. A failure is retried with jittered backoff from 5 minutes to 6 hours, never past an hour before expiry. - TLS and mutual TLS to upstreams. A cluster's new `tls` block takes `ca_file` (the pinned CA bundle, the only trust anchors), `server_name` (the SNI and certificate name, by default the endpoint's host) and `cert_file` and `key_file` (the client certificate). `edgefront_core::http1::TlsTo` loads it, and `Client::with_tls` sends over its own pool of TLS connections. The proxy's requests and health checks both go over it. The block is re-read on every reload, so certificates rotate with SIGHUP. Unreadable files fail the load, and a cluster that still cannot load its TLS refuses rather than speak cleartext (`Client::refusing`). `crates/edgefront-proxy/tests/upstream_tls.rs` covers the pinned CA, the name override, pooling, mutual TLS, rotation, an expired client certificate and the configuration rules. `e2e_port_failover.rs` runs the resilient failover drill over HTTPS with mutual TLS, and it keeps 100.00% in all four phases. Drill upstreams can serve TLS (`drill::Upstream::start_tls`). The fixture generator adds `client-rotated`. (#89)
- Mutual TLS on the listener (`edgefront_core::tls::client_auth`). `tls.client_auth` names a CA bundle (`ca_file`) and whether a certificate is `required` (the default). rustls's WebPKI verifier refuses untrusted, expired and non-client certificates in the handshake. A verified certificate's DNS and URI SANs and its SPIFFE ID (`ClientIdentity`) reach every request in `TlsInfo::client`. A cluster's new `allowed_clients` (`spiffe://` IDs or `/*` prefixes, and `dns:<name>`) admits only matching clients and answers the rest `403 client_not_allowed`. Configuration validation requires `tls.client_auth` for it. The proxy sends the verified ID upstream as `X-Client-Spiffe-Id`, and always removes a client's own value. Tests cover the valid, expired, wrong-CA and missing certificates, optional verification, the configuration rules, routing by identity, and the spoofed header. (#89)

- OCSP stapling and session ticket rotation (`edgefront_core::tls::ocsp` and `tls::tickets`). A certificate whose chain has its issuer and whose leaf names an OCSP responder gets its response stapled. The response must be fetched in the background by POST with a SHA-1 `CertID`, signed by the issuer or a delegated responder it carries, `good`, and inside its validity with five minutes of slack. It is refreshed halfway through its validity, kept with backed-off retries while the responder fails, and withdrawn once it expires. `ocsp_stapling` (on by default) and `ocsp_responder` (an `http://` override) are new `tls` settings. Staples and ticket keys are shared by every snapshot of a listener, so a reload staples at once and keeps resumption working. Ticket keys rotate every hour, with the previous key still decrypting, so a ticket resumes across one rotation and never across two. `tls::der` gains the certificate parts an OCSP request and a signature check need, and a DER writer. ring becomes a direct dependency (budget 17). `crates/edgefront-core/tests/ocsp.rs` covers the response checks and the stapler on a manual clock. `tests/tls.rs` resumes TLS 1.3 and 1.2 sessions across three rotations with reloads, and `lifecycle.rs` resumes one across a SIGHUP. The fixture generator gains `-extra` for the week's new certificates. (#89)

- `edgefront_core::l4`, pacerd's TCP data path from `src/pacerd/proxy.c` ported to tokio. It is the base L4 relay that the limiter's later W03 tasks and proxy week 12 build on.
  - `proxy` connects to the upstream within `connect_timeout`, with Nagle off on both sides. While it waits, it reads the client into the upstream-bound buffer.
  - It copies both ways through one allocation of two fixed buffers per connection. A direction reads only while its buffer has room, so TCP flow control pushes back on the sender. Unwritten bytes move to the front when the tail runs out, as in pacerd.
  - Each end of stream is passed on with `shutdown(SHUT_WR)` once the bytes before it are written. The connection is done once both directions are shut.
  - A connection on which nothing is read for `idle_timeout` is closed. As in pacerd, only reads count as activity.
  - `Outcome` gives the `CloseReason` with pacerd's `reason=` name, plus `bytes_up` and `bytes_down`. Reasons cover done, idle and connect timeouts, refused, unreachable and socket failures, and resets or errors on each side, grouped as pacerd's `closed_upstream_failed` and `closed_reset` counters group them.
  - `relay` and `proxy_with` take any `AsyncRead + AsyncWrite` streams. `RelayConfig` defaults to pacerd's 16 KiB, 5 s and 5 min.

- The keyed `Table` has no 4.5-year horizon any more either. Each shard keeps a base epoch, one behind the newest timestamp it has seen, and its entries keep arrival times as offsets from it; a timestamp two epochs past the base moves it under the shard lock, in one pass over the shard. Every `u64` timestamp is valid and decisions equal exact arithmetic on absolute time. `EntryState::tat` is now `u128` ticks since the clock's start. The table's reference model checks it at 4.5, 10 and 100 years of uptime, across epoch boundaries with keys in debt and after idle gaps, and the difftest applies pacer's clamp before calling `Table`. Table speed is unchanged. (#82)
- `Gcra` has no 4.5-year horizon any more. It stores its arrival time as an offset from a moving epoch of 2^56 ns and rebases it lock-free when the time it has to store moves on, so every `u64` timestamp is valid and decisions equal exact arithmetic on absolute time. `Gcra::tat` now returns `u128`, and `Gcra::base_epoch` is new. New module `edgefront_limiter::clock`: the epoch arithmetic, a `Clock` trait, `MonotonicClock` and `ManualClock`. The reference model checks the limiter at 4.5, 10 and 100 years of uptime, across epoch boundaries and idle gaps. - New crate `edgefront-limiter`, libpacer's algorithms in safe Rust with no dependencies (#79):
  - `Gcra`: an exact, lock-free token bucket whose arrival time is one `AtomicU64`, with `take`, `take_upto`, `take_range`, `refund`, `remaining` and `reconfigure`, and retry-after times exact to the nanosecond.
  - `Table`: per-key buckets in fixed memory, sharded, with idle reclaim, deny or evict when full, holds, sweeps and stats. Keys are hashed with SipHash-1-3 by default, or SipHash-2-4 to place keys as libpacer does.
  - `Window`: a sliding-window counter for quotas.
- The keyed table takes about 60% more decisions per second than libpacer's with spread keys and 25% more with one hot key on the same machine, where it took 15 to 30% fewer before: the SipHash tail is built in registers instead of reloaded from a byte buffer, which stalled every hash on store forwarding, and table entries are aligned to cache lines. Numbers are in docs/DESIGN.md. (#79)
